Case studies tend to begin at the end. There is a brief, a beautiful final screen, and a clean line connecting the two. The mess in the middle gets edited out.

I understand why. A finished story is easier to share. It gives the work a shape. But the shape is often a little too clean, and it quietly teaches the wrong lesson: that good work arrives fully formed if you are talented enough.

The middle is where the work actually happens. It is also where most of the anxiety lives.

## The middle has no camera

At the beginning, everything is possible. At the end, the decisions have been made. The middle is the long stretch where you know enough to be worried and not enough to be certain.

This is the part I am trying to get better at showing. Not because process is automatically interesting, but because the decisions are. Why did this layout survive? What did the first version get wrong? Which constraint turned out to be useful?

A screenshot of a polished screen does not answer those questions. A small before-and-after does. A note about the thing you removed does. Even a sentence about what you argued with yourself about can be more useful than another hero image.

I have started keeping a lighter kind of record while I work. Not a polished journal, just a place for the small turns:

- The version that felt correct until it was on a real screen.
- The label that got shorter when the layout got honest.
- The idea that came back three days later with a better reason.

The record is not impressive. It is useful.

## Finish is a direction, not a feeling

The other thing I have learned about unfinished work is that it rarely announces itself as done. There is no internal bell. One day the questions get smaller, the edges stop moving, and you realize you have been circling the same two decisions for an hour.

That is usually the moment to stop. Not because the work is perfect, but because more time is no longer buying clarity.

I used to think the goal was to feel finished. Now I think the goal is to make the next decision easier. Sometimes that means simplifying the brief. Sometimes it means accepting that the strange little detail is the part with a pulse.

The work can be unfinished and still be whole. A project can be shipped and still have a question inside it. That does not make it dishonest. It makes it alive.

The case study can wait. First, let me tell you what the middle looked like.
