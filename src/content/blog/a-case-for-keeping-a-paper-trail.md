I keep a folder called fragments. It is not a system. There are screenshots with names like `maybe-this-one`, half-written sentences, color tests, and links I saved because they made me feel something I could not explain yet.

Most of it will never become anything. A few pieces will turn up months later in a project that has nothing to do with the folder. That is the point.

The paper trail is not there to make me productive. It is there to keep me from pretending that my memory is better than it is.

## Write it down before it makes sense

The useful notes are rarely the tidy ones. They are the notes made in the middle of a thought, before the thought has been trained to sound reasonable.

I have started writing down the reason behind a decision while I am still unsure about it. Not the final rationale I would give a client. The real one: this felt too heavy, this needed air, this color made the page feel like it was leaning forward.

Those notes are not always flattering. They reveal how much of design is a conversation between instinct and evidence. That is useful. A decision that only looks inevitable in hindsight is harder to learn from.

There is a practical side too. A small trail of screenshots and sentences makes it easier to return to a project after a week away. I can see what I was trying to protect, instead of guessing from the final layer names.

## A record of attention

A paper trail is also a record of what you noticed. The thing you kept coming back to. The question you asked three times in different ways.

I do not think every project needs a beautiful process wall. I do think it needs a place where the thinking can be a little unfinished. A place that is allowed to be messy because it is not for anyone else yet.

The folder keeps me honest. It reminds me that the clean version on the screen is one small part of a much longer conversation. And it leaves a little breadcrumb for the next time I am stuck.

Write it down before it makes sense. You can decide what it means later.
